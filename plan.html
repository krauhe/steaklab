1. The experience

The aim is to understand a particular cut: choose its geometry and ingredient, set a desired result, then explore the effects of heat, oil, turning and resting. Physics, visual appeal and responsiveness all matter. Every user-facing parameter has contextual help.

See

A 28-degree orthographic pan/steak view with physical millimetre coordinates and visible thickness. The front section uses the same horizontal/vertical scale without height exaggeration. Labeled isotherms, optional element mesh, A/B labels attached to the meat and a browning view. The handle and separate browning/carryover cards were removed to give the two graphs more space.

Experiment

A compact tool palette aligns with the action canvas. Every action has its own icon; no grouping or dedicated oil lane. Horizontal position defines time, with a vertical guide while dragging. Click for an anchored popup: heat and oil use immediate sliders; rest offers three methods. Disable or remove individual actions. Clear has Undo. Editing preserves the viewing time. Arrow keys move cards by 5 seconds (Shift: 30).

Understand

A fixed through-thickness profile above the pan, face A, face B and center histories. B stays at the top and A at the bottom; temperature axes remain fixed during playback. Edits preserve the selected viewing time. coldest point, heat differences, evaporation and carryover.

The visual direction uses dark olive, warm neutrals and a pale green accent. Graphics are drawn locally in canvas, including the steak texture. At HD sizes, controls are on the left, the scene and plan in the center, and both graphs on the right, without page scrolling. Narrow screens use a scrolling layout. No external images, fonts or chart libraries are required.

3. Physics in this version

The meat is an equivalent cylinder with radius R = √(a·b), where a and b are the ellipse's semiaxes. This preserves volume and contact area. A perimeter correction adjusts lateral heat loss. It does not reproduce different conduction distances along the two axes of a true ellipse.

The mesh has 19 radial nodes and 25 thickness nodes: 475 nodes and 864 linear triangular elements. Heat conduction follows ρc ∂T/∂t = ∇·(k∇T), weighted by 2πr.

For each triangle of area A:
Kᵢⱼ = k · 2π · A · r̄ · (∇Nᵢ · ∇Nⱼ)
Mᵢ  = ρc · 2π · A · (2rᵢ + rⱼ + rₖ) / 12

Each conduction edge transfers gᵢⱼ · (Tⱼ − Tᵢ), with gᵢⱼ = −Kᵢⱼ.
The same heat enters one endpoint and leaves the other.

Explicit time step ≤ 0.2 s, reduced using the conductance row sums,
surface convection/radiation, contact coupling and pan heat capacity.

The mass expression integrates the axisymmetric shape functions exactly. The numerical tests confirm total heat capacity, exposed areas and closed-system energy conservation.

4. Suggested cooking plans

  1. Read ingredient, thickness, diameter, ellipse ratio, starting temperature, pan, moisture, contact and desired doneness/core target.
  2. Simulate preheating to an ingredient-specific model temperature, then add oil and meat.
  3. Reduce power after the initial sear and flip every minute.
  4. Clone the complete physical state to try a prospective rest without changing the cooking simulation.
  5. Search for the first candidate whose predicted peak center temperature during the rest reaches the target. The rest lasts 60–180 seconds depending on size and ingredient. Suggested plans initially use open-air resting; subsequent manual changes invalidate the displayed forecast.
  6. Place the proposed actions on the editable timeline, show expected removal and peak temperatures, and replay the same engine to generate the visual result.

If the model does not reach the goal within 45 minutes, the interface reports that limit and shows the trial plan. Suggestions use a fixed family of sear/finish/flip strategies; they do not globally optimize crust, juiciness or energy use. Small target overshoots can arise from the 5-second candidate spacing.

A suggestion targets the simulated geometric center. It does not establish safe real cooking or certify the coldest point. The summary exposes the model's coldest temperature at removal, and official guidance remains visible. A rare-beef culinary target differs from conservative minimum-temperature guidance.

6. Verification

Automated checks cover the coupled model. They include spatial pan conduction, burner localization, contact cooling, pan-mesh refinement, clone isolation, and heat capacity and area integration; energy conservation with coupled pan, oil, losses and latent heat; unchanged material state during a flip; natural carryover; mesh-independent water; wet-node boiling behavior; analytical axial diffusion convergence; extreme geometries; exact event timing; disabled/restored actions; air/foil/pan resting and energy conservation; non-linear color/legend agreement; chunked/synchronous equivalence; suggested-plan replay for all four ingredients; thickness response; isolated forecast clones; translation behavior; source-link integrity; and the self-contained bundle.

For an insulated cosine-mode diffusion benchmark, RMS errors were 0.01954, 0.00915 and 0.00450 °C for 12, 24 and 48 thickness intervals. The full-process energy test passes a 5 × 10−6 joule residual threshold. These are numerical consistency checks, not evidence that real cooking times are accurate.

Browser QA covers HD layout bounds, native tool dragging, unchanged lane positions after moving a card, separate actions without grouping, fixed action types, anchored slider editing, Clear/Undo, resting choices, animated playback, vertical depth and a top temperature scale, playback and seeking, parameter changes, help dialogs, plan editing and invalid time rejection, side changes, resting, saved comparisons, suggested plans and narrow-screen layout. Preview testing uses the same bundled HTML over a temporary loopback server. The browser automation policy blocks direct file: navigation; offline capability is supported by the self-contained bundle check and the absence of runtime resource requests, rather than a claimed direct-file browser test.

7. The path to more realistic physics

  1. Measure and calibrate first. Collect pan/contact/core thermocouple traces for known cuts, dimensions and water loss. Fit uncertain contact and evaporation parameters; reserve separate runs for validation.
  2. Add a spatial pan. Implemented: radial bottom rings and flared-wall cells, burner footprint and local contact, with conservative energy exchange. Next: measured layered construction, through-thickness gradients and non-axisymmetric contacts.
  3. Improve water and materials. Add temperature/moisture-dependent properties, transport and pressure, then shrinkage and geometry changes. Keep a full enthalpy budget.
  4. Resolve geometry and grills. Move from equivalent circular sections to a true elliptical/3D mesh and locally resolved grill contacts. The rendering should continue to show the actual field.
  5. Extend quality and planning. Calibrated crust kinetics, uncertainty ranges, additional cooking strategies and multi-objective planning for temperature uniformity and crust.
  6. Add languages. Complete and visually check each language pack, including long labels, assistive text and food-guidance localization.
