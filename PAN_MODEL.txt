SPATIAL PAN MODEL

The pan is a rotationally symmetric thermally thin metal shell. Each bottom
annulus and wall segment has its own temperature and heat capacity. Temperature
through the metal thickness is assumed uniform; azimuthal hot spots and separate
layers of a composite pan are not resolved.

For each cell:
  C_i dT_i/dt = sum_j G_ij (T_j - T_i) + burner_i - contact_i - losses_i

Each internal conduction exchange enters the two cells with opposite signs.
Meat contact likewise exchanges equal and opposite energy between the meat FEM
nodes and the pan cells beneath the equal-area circular contact footprint.
The central burner supplies energy only inside its footprint. The wall receives
heat by conduction from the floor and loses heat to the room.

The pan mean shown in the temperature history is heat-capacity weighted. Rendering
uses the local cell temperatures. A warm mean therefore does not imply a warm rim.
Oil adds heat capacity and improves contact; its animation is not a fluid solver.

Pan-to-meat radiation retains an effective exposed-floor radiosity approximation,
not a fully resolved cell-to-cell radiation enclosure. Wall-to-meat radiation and
hot-air plumes are not resolved. Material properties are illustrative effective
constants, not measurements of the user's commercial pan.

Numerical energy conservation and refinement tests check the discretization; they
do not calibrate real cooking times. See tests/pan.test.cjs for pan-specific checks.

Theory background: COMSOL, thin-shell heat conduction formulations
https://doc.comsol.com/6.3/doc/com.comsol.help.heat/heat_ug_theory.07.030.html
